# Bispectral Subharmonic Resonance and Non-Linear Energy Transfer

## 1. Resonant Triad Geometry and the $-0.5$ Slope
In the squared auto-bicoherence difference plane $b^2(f_1, f_2)$ of Shot #88653, the dominant three-wave couplings align closely along the locus:
$$f_2 = -0.5 f_1$$

Substituting this locus into the general three-wave difference resonance condition $f_3 = f_1 + f_2$ yields:
$$f_3 = f_1 - 0.5 f_1 = 0.5 f_1$$

Hence, $|f_2| = f_3 = 0.5 f_1$. This algebraic relation defines a **Subharmonic Resonance**, wherein a higher-frequency pump wave non-linearly phase-locks with fluctuations at half its fundamental frequency.

## 2. Direction of Non-Linear Energy Transfer
Following the single-point bispectral transport formulation of Kim & Powers (1979) and Ritz et al. (1989), the sign of the imaginary part of the normalized complex bispectrum, $\text{Im}[B(f_1, f_2)]$, determines the net energy transfer direction:
* **$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] > 0$:** Upward energy transfer (coalescence / inverse cascade).
* **$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] < 0$:** Downward energy transfer (parametric decay instability).

Because difference-frequency interactions ($f_2 < 0$) are evaluated via the conjugate bispectrum relation, the sum-triad imaginary component satisfies:
$$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] = -\text{Im}[B_{\text{diff}}]$$

## 3. Statistical Significance and Sliding-Window Biphase Analysis
For an ensemble of $N = 30$ segments windowed with 50% overlapping Hanning tapers, the effective number of independent realizations is $N_{\text{eff}} \approx 15$. The corresponding 95% confidence noise significance threshold is:
$$b^2_{95} = -\frac{\ln(0.05)}{N_{\text{eff}}} = \frac{2.996}{15} \approx 0.20$$

In the stationary ensemble average of the pure NBI heating phase ($291.3\text{--}300.0\text{ ms}$), the measured bicoherence on the $-0.5$ subharmonic locus reaches **$b^2 = 0.88$**, substantially exceeding the 95% threshold.

Applying a spectral power floor threshold (excluding intervals where ambient power drops into the digitized noise floor) removes spurious phase fluctuations. A sliding-window scan ($8.0\text{ ms}$ duration, $0.25\text{ ms}$ step) evaluated across the pure NBI phase and into the beam termination window demonstrates that all high-SNR intervals consistently exhibit:
$$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] > 0$$

This confirms that the non-linear coupling is characterized by persistent upward energy transfer (coalescence) from the ~40-50 kHz branch toward the ~100 kHz fluctuation.

However, the sign is dependent on the SNR threshold. Just below the threshold cut, the biphase passes through zero near 301.5 ms. As shown below, lowering the threshold incorporates excluded windows at 296.8-301.5 ms (where $\mathrm{Im}[B_{\mathrm{sum}}] < 0$), causing the sign to become threshold-dependent:

### Mode Relationship: Amplitude Scaling Analysis
To distinguish whether the ~100 kHz oscillation is a passive second harmonic generated by the saturation of a ~50 kHz fundamental, or whether both modes represent distinct branches interacting via inverse energy transfer, the scaling relationship between their spectral amplitudes was evaluated:
* **Passive Harmonic Saturation:** Predicts quadratic amplitude scaling, $A_{100} \propto A_{50}^2$ (logarithmic slope $= 2.0$).
* **Primary Instability with Shared Drive / Feeding:** Predicts linear co-modulation, $A_{100} \propto A_{50}^{1.0}$ (logarithmic slope $= 1.0$).

Linear regression across the full 75-window dataset yields a slope of 0.81 (R² = 0.90). Minimum band powers during the plasma phase (~10⁻⁵) are comparable to quiet pre-plasma intervals, consistent with hitting a measurement noise limit in low-SNR windows. The 0.81 slope therefore likely reflects the scaling of broadband background plasma turbulence or digitizer noise rather than physical coupling. When restricted to the 16 valid high-SNR windows (den > 2.5×10⁻²³), the pooled slopes shift upward:
* **Band-Integrated / Peak Amplitude Extraction:** Pooled OLS slopes range from 2.10 (band) to 2.26 (peak).
* **Confidence Intervals:** Rigorous block-bootstrap 95% CIs (block = 8 ms) yield [0.94, 2.37] for the 16 windows. A broader selection (broadband power > median, N=37) yields [0.99, 2.34]. 

Crucially, the high pooled slope is driven by the macroscopic amplitude drop between the two episodes. Evaluated individually within each episode, the tracking slopes are lower: **1.39** (Episode 1, N=6) and **0.95** (Episode 2, N=10). Furthermore, an unrestricted triad search reveals that the two episodes are driven by completely different dynamics:
* **Episode 1:** Dominated by subharmonic phase coupling (e.g., $101.6 \leftrightarrow 50.8 + 50.8$\,kHz).
* **Episode 2:** Dominated by highly asymmetric triads (e.g., $111.3 \leftrightarrow 44.9 + 66.4$\,kHz) rather than a pure harmonic. An exploratory direct sum-triad amplitude scaling test for Episode 2 ($A_{111} \sim A_{45}A_{66}$) yields a scaling slope of just 0.54 (with fixed bins, $N=10$).

The empirical scaling is inconclusive. While the pooled slope aligns with the 2.0 prediction for quadratic harmonic generation, the linear/shared-envelope model (slope ≈ 1.0) is **not excluded**, as the long-block CIs include 1.0 and the within-episode slopes are near 1.0.

**Caveats:** 
1. **Effective N:** The windows overlap heavily (8.0 ms length, 0.25 ms step). The valid centers span only ~1.75 ms (Ep 1) and ~2.75 ms (Ep 2), so the effective number of independent samples is ≈ 2.
2. **Mode Content:** Peak frequency tracking reveals the lower band peaks at ~40-41 kHz and the upper band at ~90-105 kHz. The frequency ratio ranges from ~2.21 to 2.57 (exceeding the ~0.97 kHz Welch resolution), meaning the upper band tracks the independently validated ~89-95 kHz mode rather than exactly 2× the 40 kHz peak. A strict passive second harmonic is disfavored by this frequency ratio and the shifting triad dynamics.

## 4. Integrated (Total) Bicoherence
The total squared bicoherence integrated over a target frequency band is defined as:
$$b_{\text{total}}^2 = \sum_{(f_1, f_2) \in \text{band}} b^2(f_1, f_2)$$

### Empirical Noise Floor
Under the null hypothesis of uncoupled Gaussian noise, the sample estimator of squared bicoherence has an expected bias of $\mathbb{E}[b^2] = 1 / N_{\text{eff}}$. For the $\sim4800$ physical discrete triad pairs in the $40 \le f_3 \le 80\text{ kHz}$ band ($N_{\text{fft}} = 512$, $f_s = 1.0\text{ MHz}$), the expected noise baseline is assumed to be $\sim 320$ for $N_{\text{eff}} \approx 15$. 
Empirically, the total bicoherence computed in quiet-time windows without significant MHD activity (e.g., 400-410 ms) is 293-307 (for 30 segments, $N_{\text{eff}} \approx 15$), and 491-535 in the 150-160 ms window ($N_{\text{eff}} \approx 9-10$).

### Physical Interpretation: Localized Ridge vs. Phase Mixing
1. **Spectral Localization:** During the pure NBI phase, the band-integrated total bicoherence ($349.88$) modestly exceeds the broad statistical noise baseline ($1.09\times$), while localized individual triads along the subharmonic ridge reach $b^2 = 0.88$ ($>4\times$ the individual 95% threshold of $0.20$). This confirms that the non-linear coupling is concentrated along a narrow resonant line rather than distributed broadband across the entire band.
2. **Macroscopic Phase Interference:** Extending the analysis window to the macroscopic $40\text{ ms}$ burst causes the aggregate bicoherence to drop to $\sim 38$ (with a theoretical floor around 64). Over short $\sim 8\text{ ms}$ intervals, the triad phase $\Phi = \theta_1 + \theta_2 - \theta_3$ remains coherent. However, across longer observation times, successive frequency transitions may introduce phase slips. While falling at or below the floor (which has $\sim 1.7\times$ uncertainty) is consistent with time-averaged phase cancellation due to intermittent avalanching, it is not a definitive proof.
