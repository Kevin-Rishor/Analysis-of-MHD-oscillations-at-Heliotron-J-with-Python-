# Bispectral Subharmonic Resonance and Non-Linear Energy Transfer

## 1. Resonant Triad Geometry and the $-0.5$ Slope
In the squared auto-bicoherence difference plane $b^2(f_1, f_2)$ of Shot #88653, the dominant three-wave couplings align closely along the locus:
$$f_2 = -0.5 f_1$$

Substituting this locus into the general three-wave difference resonance condition $f_3 = f_1 + f_2$ yields:
$$f_3 = f_1 - 0.5 f_1 = 0.5 f_1$$

Hence, $|f_2| = f_3 = 0.5 f_1$. This algebraic relation defines a **Subharmonic Resonance**, wherein a higher-frequency pump wave non-linearly phase-locks with fluctuations at half its fundamental frequency.

## 2. Direction of Non-Linear Energy Transfer
Following the single-point bispectral transport formulation of Kim & Powers (1979) and Ritz et al. (1989), the sign of the imaginary part of the normalized complex bispectrum, $\text{Im}[B(f_1, f_2)]$, determines the net energy transfer direction:
* **$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] > 0$:** Upward energy transfer (coalescence / inverse cascade).
* **$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] < 0$:** Downward energy transfer (parametric decay instability).

Because difference-frequency interactions ($f_2 < 0$) are evaluated via the conjugate bispectrum relation, the sum-triad imaginary component satisfies:
$$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] = -\text{Im}[B_{\text{diff}}]$$

## 3. Statistical Significance and Sliding-Window Biphase Analysis
For an ensemble of $N = 30$ segments windowed with 50% overlapping Hanning tapers, the effective number of independent realizations is $N_{\text{eff}} \approx 15$. The corresponding 95% confidence noise significance threshold is:
$$b^2_{95} = -\frac{\ln(0.05)}{N_{\text{eff}}} = \frac{2.996}{15} \approx 0.20$$

In the stationary ensemble average of the pure NBI heating phase ($291.3\text{--}300.0\text{ ms}$), the measured bicoherence on the $-0.5$ subharmonic locus reaches **$b^2 = 0.88$**, substantially exceeding the 95% threshold.

Applying a spectral power floor threshold (excluding intervals where ambient power drops into the digitized noise floor) removes spurious phase fluctuations. A sliding-window scan ($8.0\text{ ms}$ duration, $0.25\text{ ms}$ step) evaluated across the pure NBI phase and into the beam termination window demonstrates that all high-SNR intervals consistently exhibit:
$$\text{Im}[B_{\text{sum}}] > 0$$

This confirms that the non-linear coupling is characterized by persistent upward energy transfer (coalescence) from the ~50 kHz branch toward the ~100 kHz fluctuation.

### Mode Relationship: Amplitude Scaling Analysis
To distinguish whether the ~100 kHz oscillation is a passive second harmonic generated by the saturation of a ~50 kHz fundamental, or whether both modes represent distinct branches interacting via inverse energy transfer, the scaling relationship between their spectral amplitudes was evaluated:
* **Passive Harmonic Saturation:** Predicts quadratic amplitude scaling, $A_{100} \propto A_{50}^2$ (logarithmic slope $= 2.0$).
* **Primary Instability with Shared Drive / Feeding:** Predicts linear co-modulation, $A_{100} \propto A_{50}^{1.0}$ (logarithmic slope $= 1.0$).

Linear regression across the full 75-window dataset yields a slope of 0.81 (R² = 0.90), but this is biased by the inclusion of low-SNR intervals. When restricted to the 16 valid high-SNR windows (den > 2.5×10⁻²³), the slopes shift upward:
* **Band-Integrated Amplitude Extraction:** OLS slope = 2.10, RMA slope = 2.15 (R² = 0.95; block-bootstrap 95% CI: [0.96, 2.63])
* **Peak Amplitude Extraction:** OLS slope = 2.26, RMA slope = 2.34 (R² = 0.94; block-bootstrap 95% CI: [0.69, 2.79])

Using an independent window-selection criterion (broadband power > median, N = 37 windows), the slopes are 2.26 (band) and 2.39 (peak) with tighter CIs: [2.12, 2.54] and [2.07, 2.64] respectively, excluding slope = 1.0 at 95% confidence.

The empirical scaling exponent of ~2.1–2.3 is consistent with the 2.0 prediction for quadratic harmonic generation. This is consistent with the ~100 kHz fluctuation being a **passive second harmonic** generated by the non-linear saturation of the ~50 kHz fundamental instability. The linear/shared-envelope model (slope ≈ 1) is disfavored but not fully excluded by the 16-window bootstrap CI.

**Caveats:** (1) The 16 valid windows overlap by 7.75/8.0 ms, so the effective number of independent samples is ~4; the block-bootstrap CI accounts for this. (2) The `den` threshold contains the same 50 and 100 kHz powers being regressed — an independent broadband-power selection gives consistent slopes but tighter CIs, mitigating this concern.

## 4. Integrated (Total) Bicoherence
The total squared bicoherence integrated over a target frequency band is defined as:
$$b_{\text{total}}^2 = \sum_{(f_1, f_2) \in \text{band}} b^2(f_1, f_2)$$

### Theoretical Noise Expectation
Under the null hypothesis of uncoupled Gaussian noise, the sample estimator of squared bicoherence has an expected bias of $\mathbb{E}[b^2] = 1 / N_{\text{eff}}$. For $N_{\text{bins}} = 4,800$ discrete triad pairs in the $40 \le f_3 \le 80\text{ kHz}$ band ($N_{\text{fft}} = 512$, $f_s = 1.0\text{ MHz}$), the expected noise baseline is:
$$\mathbb{E}[b_{\text{total, noise}}^2] = \frac{N_{\text{bins}}}{N_{\text{eff}}} = \frac{4,800}{15} = 320.0$$

### Physical Interpretation: Localized Ridge vs. Phase Mixing
1. **Spectral Localization:** During the pure NBI phase, the band-integrated total bicoherence ($349.88$) modestly exceeds the broad statistical noise baseline ($1.09\times$), while localized individual triads along the subharmonic ridge reach $b^2 = 0.88$ ($>4\times$ the individual 95% threshold of $0.20$). This confirms that the non-linear coupling is concentrated along a narrow resonant line rather than distributed broadband across the entire band.
2. **Macroscopic Destructive Phase Interference:** Extending the analysis window to the macroscopic $40\text{ ms}$ burst causes the aggregate bicoherence to collapse below the noise floor ($\sim 38$ vs. $64.0$). Over short $\sim 8\text{ ms}$ intervals, the triad phase $\Phi = \theta_1 + \theta_2 - \theta_3$ remains coherent. However, across longer observation times, successive frequency-hopping transitions introduce phase slips that wash out the time-averaged coherence ($\langle e^{i \Phi} \rangle \to 0$).
